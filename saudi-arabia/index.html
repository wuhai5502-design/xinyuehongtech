What changes when the kiosk ships to Saudi Arabia

A kiosk built for a European hospital and one built for a Riyadh service centre can use the same cabinet and the same reader stack. What changes is the compliance file, the interface language, the printing and the power. Those four items decide whether the unit clears customs and whether staff can use it on day one.

The conformity route is the one buyers underestimate. Electrical products entering Saudi Arabia go through the SABER platform: a Product Certificate of Conformity is issued for the model, and a Shipment Certificate is issued per consignment. Both are tied to the importer of record, not to the factory. A quotation that does not mention this is not a complete quotation.

Arabic is not a translation task. The interface runs right-to-left, which changes the layout of forms and the position of navigation, and the receipt printer has to carry an Arabic code page in its firmware. A terminal that prints a mangled receipt in Arabic will be switched off by the counter staff within a week.

Finally, the power. Saudi Arabia runs 230 V at 60 Hz, and socket types vary between buildings, so the build is specified with the plug and the input range agreed in writing rather than with a default European cord.

SPECIFICATION

Six points to settle before the drawing

CONFORMITY

SABER, not just CE

A CE file helps the laboratory work, but entry needs the Saudi Product Certificate and a shipment certificate per consignment. Confirm who holds the importer of record before production starts.

LANGUAGE

Arabic first, RTL

Interface strings, form direction and number formatting all change. The screen size and the font size have to be re-checked with Arabic text, which is taller than Latin at the same point size.

PRINTING

Arabic code page

Thermal printers need firmware support for the Arabic character set. Ask for a printed sample in Arabic before the batch runs, not after.

POWER

230 V / 60 Hz

Socket type varies by building. The build is specified with the plug type, the input range and the surge protection agreed in writing.

PAYMENT

mada-certified devices

Card acceptance goes through a certified payment terminal, normally provided by the acquirer. The kiosk supplies mounting, power and the cable route rather than an integrated payment module.

CLIMATE

Heat and dust

Indoor malls are cool; service halls, industrial sites and covered outdoor positions are not. Ambient temperature, dust and direct sun decide the fanless platform and the panel brightness.

Where these terminals are used in the Kingdom

Government service centres. Identity-heavy transactions dominate: national ID, residency documents, fee payment and certificate printing. The government kiosk guide covers the reader bank, and the dual-screen government terminal is the usual cabinet because the upper screen carries Arabic guidance while the lower screen runs the transaction.

Hospitals and clinics. Registration, insurance eligibility and self-payment, often in halls where the queue is already long at 8 am. The hospital registration and payment kiosk is the reference build; corridors use the wall-mounted payment terminal where floor space is not available.

Restaurants and retail. Self-ordering is the highest-volume kiosk category in the region, and the countertop format is usually enough. See the tilted countertop terminal and the self-ordering category.

Banks and payment halls. Branch self-service with an encrypting PIN pad, statement printing and card issue. The bank kiosk and the ATM-style terminal cover the two common layouts.

From order to customs clearance

  1. Specification. Module list, Arabic interface requirements, plug type and ambient conditions confirmed in writing.
  2. Product Certificate. The model is registered through SABER and the Product CoC is issued. This is a prerequisite, not a formality.
  3. Production and testing. Standard lead time is 15 days from deposit and approved drawing inside our standard module set, including a 12-hour burn-in.
  4. Shipment Certificate. Issued per consignment against the commercial invoice and packing list.
  5. Freight. Sea freight from Shenzhen to Jeddah, King Abdullah Port or Dammam, typically three to four weeks door to port, plus inland delivery.
  6. Clearance and handover. The importer of record clears the goods; we supply the documents, the Arabic labelling artwork and the test report.

Budget the conformity work as a project cost rather than a per-unit cost: it is paid once per model family, and it is the line most often missing when buyers compare a Chinese quote with a local distributor's price. The cost guide sets out the other lines that are usually excluded.

Questions from Saudi buyers

Do you provide SASO or SABER certification?
We support the process with test reports, technical files and Arabic labelling artwork, and we work with the certificate body your importer uses. The Product Certificate and the Shipment Certificate are issued to the importer of record, so the certificate route is agreed before production rather than after.
Can the interface and the receipt be in Arabic?
Yes. The application layer handles the right-to-left layout, and the thermal printer is specified with Arabic code page support. We ask for a printed Arabic sample before the batch runs so the character rendering is confirmed on paper, not just on screen.
What voltage and plug type are supplied?
Saudi Arabia is 230 V at 60 Hz. Plug type varies between buildings, so the cord and plug are agreed per project, together with the input range and surge protection.
How long does shipping take?
Sea freight from Shenzhen to Jeddah, King Abdullah Port or Dammam is typically three to four weeks to port, plus inland delivery and clearance. Air freight is used for samples and pilot units.
Can we start with a small quantity?
Yes. Minimum order quantity is one unit for standard cabinets, and pilot units go through the same burn-in and inspection route as a production batch, so the sample represents what the batch will be.
