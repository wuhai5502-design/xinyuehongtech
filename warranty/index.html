Warranty and After-Sales Policy

What is checked before a terminal leaves the factory, what a warranty covers on a self-service kiosk, and how a claim is handled once the machine is in service.

Why there is no single warranty figure on this page: a kiosk carries modules from different vendors — printer, card reader, touch panel, payment terminal, computer — and each comes with its own term. Quoting one number for the whole machine would mislead. The term per module, the spares list, the response time and who pays return freight are fixed in the quotation and the contract, in writing.

What is checked before shipping

  • Incoming module check on arrival at the factory, before the module is installed.
  • Assembly and wiring inspection, including connector seating and cable routing.
  • Full-function test on the assembled unit: display, touch, printer, scanner, readers and payment bracket.
  • 12-hour burn-in with the machine powered and the main functions cycling, to catch early failures.
  • Final quality check (FQC) and photographic record before packing.

Every unit carries a serial number. The test record and the photographs are kept against that serial, so a later claim can be traced to the build.

What the warranty covers

A kiosk warranty is written module by module, because the parts fail in different ways:

  • Modules supplied by us — printer, card reader, barcode scanner, camera, fingerprint sensor, power supply, computer and display — covered for the term stated per module in the quotation.
  • The enclosure and the assembly — workmanship, welding, powder coat and wiring — covered against manufacturing defects.
  • A certified payment terminal — its warranty and its certification stay with the terminal vendor or the acquirer, not with the cabinet. We cover the bracket, cut-out, power and cable route.
  • Customer-supplied modules — the module’s warranty stays on the customer’s account with the module vendor. We cover the integration around it.

What voids or limits cover

  • Physical damage, liquid ingress beyond the stated IP rating, or operation outside the rated temperature range.
  • Unauthorised firmware, software or hardware modification, including a different paper stock than the approved one.
  • Consumables: thermal paper, receipt rolls, cleaning supplies and fuses.
  • Damage in transit that was not recorded on the carrier’s delivery receipt.

The report should carry the serial number, the symptom, when it started, and a photograph or short video. We reply within 24 hours with a diagnosis step: which module to test, which connector to reseat, which log to export. If the module is at fault, the resolution is a replacement module from spares, a return for repair, or a field swap, according to what the contract states for that module and destination.

Spares and response time

Because a warranty with a slow swap is still a slow machine, the quotation states the replacement lead time per module alongside the warranty term. For fleets, a spares kit is quoted with the first order so a failed printer or card reader does not wait for an international shipment.

What is not included

On-site labour, travel and local taxes are quoted separately unless the contract states otherwise. Consumables and preventive maintenance visits are quoted per project.

Talk to us about a project

If a terminal is in service and you need a spare, a diagnosis or a replacement, write to sales@xinyuehongtech.com with the serial number. If you are specifying a new project, the warranty structure is agreed with the quotation, and the procurement guide lists the points to put in writing.
