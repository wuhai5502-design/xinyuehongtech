SABER Certification for Kiosk Imports into Saudi Arabia

By Mark, Project Engineer · Published November 27, 2026 · 6 min read

In one line: A kiosk imported into Saudi Arabia goes through SABER, the Saudi conformity platform, as two certificates: a Product Certificate of Conformity for the model, and a Shipment Certificate of Conformity for each consignment. Both are issued through the importer of record, not the factory, which is why the buyer’s local partner matters as much as the hardware.

Two certificates, one platform

SABER, run by the Saudi Standards, Metrology and Quality Organization (SASO), is the online system where electrical products get their conformity documents. A kiosk project meets it twice:

  • Product Certificate of Conformity (PCoC). Issued once for the model, valid for a defined period, based on a technical file, a risk assessment and the relevant test reports.
  • Shipment Certificate of Conformity (SCoC). Issued per consignment, quoting the PCoC, so that each actual shipment clears customs.

The two are separate because a model can be approved while one specific shipment is not, for example when it contains a different plug, a changed component or a wrong quantity against the PCoC.

Who holds what

The conformity documents are tied to the importer of record, which is the Saudi-based entity that clears the goods. The factory supplies the technical file and the test reports, but it cannot hold the PCoC on the buyer’s behalf. A quotation that does not state who will obtain the certificates — and on whose account — is not a complete quotation.

This is the part that changes the commercial terms, not the hardware: the buyer needs a registered importer, and the lead time for the certificate has to sit in the project plan alongside production.

What changes in the build itself

Conformity is only half of a Saudi project. Three build-level items usually travel with it:

  • Power. 230 V / 60 Hz mains, with the plug and the internal wiring matched to the Saudi standard.
  • Interface and printing. Arabic interface running right-to-left, and a receipt printer with an Arabic code page in its firmware, so the receipt is legible rather than mangled.
  • Payment. Local payment methods such as mada, arranged through the acquirer, with the terminal mounted on the kiosk rather than built into it.

Documents to prepare before ordering

  • Technical file for the exact model: specifications, circuit and component information, and the safety and EMC test reports.
  • The importer’s registration on SABER, and a decision on who applies for the PCoC and the SCoC.
  • HS code classification, which determines the conformity route and the duty.
  • Module certificates for anything inside the kiosk with its own approval, such as the payment terminal or a wireless module.
  • Arabic documentation for the interface, the receipt and the operator manual.

SABER questions

Is SABER the same as a CE certificate?
No. CE is the European route; SABER is the Saudi route run through SASO. A CE report can support the SABER technical file, but it does not replace the Product and Shipment certificates, which are issued on the SABER platform for the Saudi market.
Who applies for the certificates, the factory or the buyer?
The Saudi importer of record applies, because the certificates are registered to that entity. The factory supplies the technical file, test reports and model information. The split of cost and responsibility belongs in the quotation.
How long does SABER take?
SABER lead time depends on the model, the completeness of the technical file and whether test reports are already available. Budget it as a project line with its own lead time, and start it in parallel with production rather than after the unit is built.
Does a changed component invalidate the certificate?
A component change can invalidate the certificate: a different power supply, plug, display or wireless module changes the technical file, so changes after the PCoC is issued must be flagged to the conformity partner before production, not after.
What about the payment terminal?
The terminal keeps its own certification and is usually arranged through the acquirer for mada. The kiosk provides the cut-out, bracket, power and cable route, and the SABER file covers the assembled unit with that terminal mounted.
