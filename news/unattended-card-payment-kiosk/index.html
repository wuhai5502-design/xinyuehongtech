Unattended Card Payment on a Kiosk

By Mark · Published November 20, 2026 · 8 min read

Quick answer: An unattended kiosk needs a payment terminal the acquirer has approved for unattended use, mounted so the keypad is reachable and the cable cannot be pulled, plus a fallback path for the moment the network drops. The kiosk provides the aperture, bracket, power and cable route; the PCI PTS approval and the merchant approval stay with the terminal and the acquirer.

Unattended is a different approval, not a different bracket

A terminal approved for countertop use is not automatically approved for a lane with no member of staff present. Unattended operation changes the risk profile: there is no clerk to check the card, no one to void a transaction, and the device sits in public view for hours. The acquirer has to confirm that the specific model is supported in unattended mode, and that confirmation belongs in writing before the cabinet is drawn.

The mounting decides the transaction

The terminal is mounted on the cabinet, not built into it, because the device keeps its own approval and its own replacement cycle. That means a cut-out sized to the exact model, a metal bracket that takes the force of a customer leaning on the keypad, and a cable route that cannot be unplugged from outside.

Three mounting details are worth drawing explicitly: the keypad angle, so the PIN shield is not defeated by a tall user looking down; the privacy hood, where the queue stands within a metre; and the service access, because a terminal that needs a screwdriver and a removed side panel to swap will slow every repair.

Power, network and what happens when it drops

Run the terminal from the kiosk supply through the bracket, with a short cable that cannot be pulled out from the customer side. Surge protection matters more than it does at a counter, because an outdoor or kerbside unit shares its feed with lighting and gates.

For the network, decide the failure behaviour before the hardware is ordered. Most projects fail closed: the screen says payment is unavailable and offers the nearest staffed point. Store-and-forward is a decision for the payment provider and the acquirer, and it is not something a cabinet can add later.

Receipts, refunds and reconciliation

An 80 mm printer with an auto-cutter covers the ordinary receipt. Where the receipt is a tax document, the paper grade and the retention rule change, and a duplicate copy needs a policy rather than a second button. Refunds stay in the payment platform; the kiosk shows the policy and routes the customer to staff.

Reconciliation is the part that tends to be discovered late: someone has to close the batch, and on an unattended unit that someone is remote. Ask who owns settlement, how the terminal reports, and what the daily close procedure looks like before the unit ships.

Where attended and unattended payment differ.

ItemAttended counterUnattended kiosk
Device approvalStandard countertop terminalModel explicitly approved for unattended use
Failure handlingClerk retries or takes another methodScreen message plus a defined alternative route
MountingCable on a standBracket, cut-out, cable locked inside the cabinet
ReconciliationManual close at the counterRemote close with a written daily procedure
ReceiptsHanded overPrinted at the unit, with a duplicate policy

What to send with the enquiry

The terminal model and the acquirer’s unattended confirmation, the payment methods to accept, the network available at the site, and who closes the batch each day. Those four answers decide the bracket, the printer position and the fallback message.

Questions integrators ask

Can we use a standard countertop terminal in a kiosk?
Only if the acquirer confirms it for unattended use. Putting a countertop terminal inside a cabinet does not change what it is approved for, and an unsupported deployment puts the merchant agreement at risk rather than the hardware.
Who certifies the payment part of the kiosk?
The terminal vendor holds the PCI PTS approval and the acquirer approves the merchant integration. The kiosk is the host: mounting, power, aperture and cable route. No cabinet maker can certify a payment flow it does not operate.
What if the customer’s card is declined?
The terminal reports it; the kiosk shows a neutral message and offers the alternative path. Do not let the application guess why a transaction failed, because the terminal is the only party that knows.
Can the kiosk take cash as well?
Yes, with an acceptor or recycler, but cash adds depth, a vault, a jam-clearing door and a collection routine. It is a service decision before it is a hardware one, and it changes the reconciliation procedure.
How much space does the terminal need inside the cabinet?
Enough for the device body, its connector, a service loop and the bracket fixing. The installation drawing from the terminal vendor is the only reliable source for that number, which is why it is requested before the sheet metal is cut.
