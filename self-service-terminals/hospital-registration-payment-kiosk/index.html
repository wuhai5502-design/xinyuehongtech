Outpatient hall, first contact

In an outpatient hall the kiosk is usually the patient's first contact with the hospital that day, and the queue behind them is the constraint. This build pairs a 32-inch working screen with a 21.5-inch guidance screen above it, so appointment check-in, registration and payment can run while the guidance screen shows the queue number and the direction to the consulting room.

The retractable keyboard with stainless-steel keycaps is there for the cases a touchscreen handles badly: an ID number, a phone number, a date of birth entered by an older patient. It retracts flush when not in use, which keeps the front face clean enough to wipe down between users.

Payment hardware covers IC, ID and bank cards with a contactless option, and the 80 mm printer issues the slip that carries the queue number. Consumables are reached from the front, so a ward clerk can change paper without moving the cabinet or calling an engineer.

Before you request a quote

Why does the unit need a keyboard if it has a touchscreen?
Numeric entry on a touchscreen is slow and error-prone for older patients and for long ID numbers. The retractable keyboard is used for those fields only, then retracts so the surface can be cleaned and the interface stays simple.
Can the guidance screen show the patient's queue position?
Yes. The upper display is driven independently of the main screen, so it can show the queue number, the waiting area and the room to go to while the lower screen completes registration or payment.
How is the unit cleaned between patients?
The front face is flat glass and stainless steel with a retractable keyboard, so there are few crevices to trap residue. Cleaning materials and frequency are set by the hospital's infection-control policy; the design simply avoids surfaces that cannot be wiped.
