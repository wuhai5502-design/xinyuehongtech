Security first, then service

The design of this unit starts from the assumption that it will hold value. The 19-inch screen is recessed into a deep fascia with an anti-peep surround, the encrypting PIN pad sits on its own secure module, and the cabinet uses a vault-style service door with mounting points for an alarm or a security seal. Those choices cost screen area and cabinet depth, and they are the reason the unit looks the way it does.

Functionally it is a cashless or cash-handling terminal depending on the module fitted: EMV chip and magnetic-stripe reading as standard, contactless as an option, and a cash acceptor or document aperture behind the secure fascia where the branch needs deposit or withdrawal. Card issuance and PIN change are the other common uses.

It suits a self-service corner inside an existing branch rather than a standalone lobby, because the plinth can be bolted down and the service access is designed for a two-person cash-handling procedure. Where the site has no secure back room, a cash-handling build should not be specified at all.

Before you request a quote

Is the recessed screen necessary for a cashless deployment?
Not strictly. The recess and the anti-peep surround exist for transactions where a balance or PIN entry could be overlooked. For a cardless enquiry terminal in a supervised lobby, a flatter fascia is available, but it is a different cabinet drawing.
Can it be bolted down and alarmed?
Yes. The plinth has a bolt-down option and the cabinet includes mounting points for an alarm contact or security seal. The exact hardware is supplied by your security contractor; we provide the mounting provisions and the cable route.
What cash modules can be fitted?
A cash acceptor, a recycler or a document aperture can be fitted behind the secure fascia, depending on the note handling your branch requires. Cash modules change the cabinet depth and the certification scope, so they are quoted as a defined variant rather than a line-item option.
