Card issue at a community clinic

This build exists for one task that most kiosks cannot do: hand a physical card to the patient. The motorised hopper holds around 100 cards and reports empty and jam states, so clinic staff know when to reload without opening the cabinet to look. That matters in a community clinic where the terminal is checked once in the morning and once at closing.

Registration and card issue are usually the first two steps of a visit, and both are on this unit: contactless and ID reading identify the patient, the dispenser issues the card, and the 80 mm printer produces the slip that carries the queue number. A patient who arrives without an appointment can complete the whole sequence at the machine.

The cabinet is cold-rolled steel with a lockable service door, sized to sit in a waiting area rather than a back office. Replenishment is a front-door operation: the hopper pulls out, a new stack drops in, and the sensor confirms the count before the unit returns to service.

Before you request a quote

How often does the card hopper need refilling?
Capacity is about 100 cards. In a small clinic that is typically a weekly task; at a busy registration desk it may be daily. The empty-state sensor reports to your application so the refill can be scheduled rather than discovered by a patient.
What happens to a card if the patient walks away before collecting it?
The dispenser can be driven to retract an uncollected card, and the event is reported to the application. Whether the card is retracted, retained or voided is a software decision, so the behaviour is agreed with your registration system during integration.
Can the kiosk read a patient's existing card as well as issue a new one?
Yes. The reference build pairs the dispenser with a contactless reader and an ID card reader, so returning patients can be identified and new cards issued from the same unit. The reader set is confirmed against the card technologies your clinic actually uses.
