Document-led service in a municipal hall

Most government service transactions begin with a document rather than a card. This build puts an ID document reader with MRZ and second-generation ID support at the centre of the unit, then surrounds it with the card readers a municipal hall actually uses: social-security card, bank card and a contactless pad for the payment step.

The 27-inch portrait screen is tall enough to show a form or a certificate preview above the reader deck without the user having to step back, which matters in a hall where the next person in the queue is standing close behind. The 80 mm printer issues receipts and short certificates; longer documents are usually printed at a staffed counter.

Because the same cabinet is deployed by different agencies, the reader set is the variable. A social-security counter may need only the card slot, while a records desk needs the document reader and a scanner. The cabinet and the harness accept both without a re-draw, which keeps spares and training consistent across a hall.

Before you request a quote

Which identity documents can the reader handle?
The reference build supports MRZ reading and second-generation ID cards. Passport chip reading depends on the reader module selected, so send the document types your service accepts and the reader will be specified against that list rather than a generic standard.
Is the scanned document stored on the kiosk?
The kiosk passes the read result to your application and does not retain a copy locally. Whether an image is stored, and for how long, is decided by your service's data policy and implemented in the software layer, not in the terminal.
Can it print certificates, or only receipts?
The 80 mm printer is intended for receipts, queue slips and short confirmations. Full-page certificates need an A4 module, which is available in the larger cabinets rather than in this body; for a hall that issues certificates, that is the model to specify.
