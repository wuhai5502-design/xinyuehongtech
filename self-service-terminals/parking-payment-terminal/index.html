Kerbside hardware, not lobby hardware

A payment terminal in a car park is judged by what happens on a wet Tuesday, not by its screen. This build puts the hardware behind a steel enclosure with a weather-resistant front option, mounts it on a floor plinth for kerbside or lane-side installation, and angles the payment deck so a driver standing at the machine can present a card, a phone or a ticket without bending down.

The 21.5-inch portrait display carries the amount due and the exit instruction, with anti-glare glass because most parking machines face a road or a windscreen. The scanner reads 1D barcodes and 2D QR codes for printed tickets and app-based parking codes, so the same unit serves both paper and phone users.

The design constraint that shapes everything else is the deck: card reader, contactless pad and ticket slot are grouped on one angled surface at a single reach point. Splitting them across the front face would force the driver to step back and lean in, which is how cards get left in readers.

Before you request a quote

What level of weather protection does the cabinet provide?
The enclosure is steel with a weather-resistant front option intended for covered kerbside and lane-side positions. The exact ingress rating for your site is confirmed against the installation drawing, because driving rain angle and coastal salt exposure change the requirement.
Can it take payment by phone as well as card?
Yes. The payment deck carries a contactless pad alongside the card reader, and the QR scanner reads parking codes from apps. Which payment methods are live depends on the acquirer and the software running on the terminal.
How is it fixed to the ground?
The unit stands on a floor plinth with a bolt-down option for permanent kerbside positions. A free-standing base is possible for temporary car parks, but it is not the default because a payment terminal is an obvious target for being pushed or struck.
