How the island works in a checkpoint hall

Checkpoint halls rarely handle one credential type. A single queue can contain ID cards, bank cards, permits and travel documents, and the four reader slots on this island are there to absorb that mix instead of sending people to different machines. Which reader goes in which slot is decided per project, so the module set follows the credential mix of the hall rather than the other way round.

The 21.5-inch guidance display above the 32-inch working screen is not decoration. It carries the next-step instruction, the lane status and the notice that would otherwise need a member of staff standing beside the unit, which is what makes a multi-step process work without supervision.

The cabinet is an island rather than a pedestal: consumables, cabling and the service panels sit inside the body, and the base can be bolted down where the hall layout is fixed. The full-size keyboard and contactless pad on the counter deck are positioned for the standing posture a traveller takes when they arrive with a bag in one hand.

Before you request a quote

Can the four reader slots be configured for our own credential mix?
Yes. Slots are assigned during specification: for example ID plus bank card plus contactless, with a document reader in the fourth position. The mechanical cut-outs and the internal harness are drawn to that assignment, so the mix is fixed before production rather than changed on site.
Why is the unit bolt-down rather than mobile?
A checkpoint island is usually placed against a lane or counter that does not move, and a bolted base removes the risk of the unit being pushed out of alignment with the queue barriers. A castor base is available for temporary halls, but it is a different cabinet specification.
What happens if a traveller's document is rejected mid-process?
The application returns to the guidance screen with the failing step highlighted, and the reader slot remains available for a second attempt. Referral to a staffed counter is handled by your software, not by the hardware; the kiosk only needs to expose the reader state to it.
