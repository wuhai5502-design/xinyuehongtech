OEM / ODM SERVICES

OEM & ODM Self-Service Kiosk Projects

Two routes to a self-service terminal that carries your own brand: adapt one of our existing kiosk platforms, or develop a new enclosure and hardware set around your brief. Both start with a written specification.

OEM or ODM: which route fits

The difference is how much of the product already exists.

01

OEM: your brand on a proven platform

Start from an existing kiosk platform and adjust screen size, colour, logo, peripherals and the software image. Faster to quote and to deliver because the mechanics and thermal design already work.

02

ODM: build the product around your brief

Where no existing cabinet meets the size, mounting or function you need, the enclosure, mechanical layout and peripheral set are developed together. More preparation, far more freedom.

What can be customised

Feasibility and exact scope are confirmed during the specification review, before anything is quoted as final.

01

Cabinet & appearance

Size, material, mounting, cabinet colour, logo placement and mechanical design requirements.

02

Touchscreen & computing

Display orientation, screen size, PC platform, operating system and connectivity selection.

03

Peripheral integration

Scanner, thermal printer, camera, NFC/RFID reader and compatible payment device planning.

04

Software & interfaces

Application fit, SDK/API requirements, ports, drivers and third-party back-end integration review.

05

Branding & user experience

Custom front graphics, user touch points, installation guidance and service access design.

06

Validation & rollout

Prototype review, functional testing, target-market requirements and acceptance checkpoints.

From requirement to a finished terminal

Seven stages, each ending with something you can approve in writing before the next one starts.

  1. DEFINESTAGE 01

    Requirement

    Application, target market, module list, volume, certification needs and target date written into one brief.

  2. DEFINESTAGE 02

    Feasibility & budget

    OEM or ODM route chosen; standard parts and changed parts separated, then quoted line by line.

  3. DESIGNSTAGE 03

    Enclosure design

    3D structure, module layout, cable routing, colour and artwork agreed on drawing.

  4. DESIGNSTAGE 04

    Prototype

    Sample built; Windows, Android or Linux image loaded and every embedded module driven by its driver or SDK.

  5. DESIGNSTAGE 05

    Validation

    Sample reviewed against your software, interfaces tested, and a closed change list signed off.

  6. DELIVERSTAGE 06

    Pilot run

    Process documents, ageing test and outgoing inspection criteria fixed before volume work begins.

  7. DELIVERSTAGE 07

    Production & shipping

    Batch build, unit-by-unit inspection, packing and shipping documents; repeat orders keyed to the same specification.

Stages 1–2 define the product, 3–5 build and prove it, 6–7 repeat it. No volume production starts before the sample and its interfaces are approved.

What happens at each stage

The detail behind the diagram.

1. Send the brief

OEM/ODM questions

What buyers usually ask before the first specification is written.

What is the difference between OEM and ODM here?
OEM means adapting one of our existing kiosk platforms with your branding and configuration. ODM means developing the enclosure and hardware layout to your own specification. The right route depends on size, mounting, function and volume.
What information is needed for a quotation?
Share the application, preferred reference design if any, screen size, peripherals, operating system, target country, estimated order volume and target schedule. Drawings are welcome but not required for a first discussion.
Is there a minimum order quantity?
There is no fixed number. OEM changes to an existing platform usually need far less volume than a new ODM enclosure, where tooling and setup effort have to be recovered. The practical minimum is agreed per project.
Can a kiosk integrate my own software?
Software integration depends on the selected operating system, drivers, SDK/APIs and third-party device compatibility. Share your software stack so the requirements can be assessed.
Do all kiosk configurations support payment?
No. Payment support requires a suitable certified payment terminal or solution, payment provider arrangements and compatibility checks for the target country.
Are certifications and lead times fixed?
They depend on the actual model, components, market and scope of changes. Request documentary confirmation for certification and an agreed project schedule before ordering.
