OEM / ODM SERVICES

OEM & ODM Self-Service Kiosk Manufacturer in Shenzhen

Xinyuehong is an OEM/ODM self-service kiosk manufacturer in Shenzhen. There are two routes to a terminal that carries your own brand: adapt one of our existing kiosk platforms, or develop a new enclosure and hardware set around your brief. Both start with a written specification.

OEM or ODM: which route fits

The difference is how much of the product already exists.

01

OEM: your brand on a proven platform

Start from an existing kiosk platform and adjust screen size, colour, logo, peripherals and the software image. Faster to quote and to deliver because the mechanics and thermal design already work.

02

ODM: build the product around your brief

Where no existing cabinet meets the size, mounting or function you need, the enclosure, mechanical layout and peripheral set are developed together. More preparation, far more freedom.

OEM, ODM, white label and contract manufacturing

Buyers use four terms for the same arrangement, and suppliers use them loosely. The distinction matters because it decides who owns the design, who holds the certification and how much of the product can still change after the first order.

Four sourcing terms, and what each one actually transfers.

TermWhat it meansWho owns the designWhat you can change
OEMYou define the specification and the brand; the factory builds to your documentYou, normallyEverything your specification covers, within what the factory can build
ODMThe factory already designs the product; you adapt and brand itThe factoryConfiguration, modules, finish, branding and the software image
White labelAn existing product sold under your brand with no design changeThe factoryLogo, packaging and colour only
Contract manufacturingYou own the drawings; the factory is a build partnerYouNothing beyond your drawings, except process changes agreed in writing

Most kiosk projects sit between OEM and ODM: a platform already exists, and the cabinet, the module set and the software image are adapted to the brief. That middle position is what this page describes. If you bring your own drawings we build to them, subject to a review of the modules inside and the certification path for the destination market.

What drives the cost of a custom build

  • Tooling and programming for a new enclosure, which is a one-off rather than a per-unit cost.
  • Finish: a standard powder-coat colour costs less than a matched one, and a printed front graphic costs more than a label.
  • Module count and class: a receipt printer, two card readers and a mounted payment terminal change both the metalwork and the assembly time.
  • Certification testing, quoted per model and per market, once rather than per unit.
  • Volume, because setup and programming amortise over the batch.
  • Change timing: a change made after the drawing is approved costs more than the same change made before it.

From requirement to a finished terminal

Seven stages, each ending with something you can approve in writing before the next one starts.

  1. DEFINESTAGE 01

    Requirement

    Application, target market, module list, volume, certification needs and target date written into one brief.

  2. DEFINESTAGE 02

    Feasibility & budget

    OEM or ODM route chosen; standard parts and changed parts separated, then quoted line by line.

  3. DESIGNSTAGE 03

    Enclosure design

    3D structure, module layout, cable routing, colour and artwork agreed on drawing.

  4. DESIGNSTAGE 04

    Prototype

    Sample built; Windows, Android or Linux image loaded and every embedded module driven by its driver or SDK.

  5. DESIGNSTAGE 05

    Validation

    Sample reviewed against your software, interfaces tested, and a closed change list signed off.

  6. DELIVERSTAGE 06

    Pilot run

    Process documents, ageing test and outgoing inspection criteria fixed before volume work begins.

  7. DELIVERSTAGE 07

    Production & shipping

    Batch build, unit-by-unit inspection, packing and shipping documents; repeat orders keyed to the same specification.

Stages 1–2 define the product, 3–5 build and prove it, 6–7 repeat it. No volume production starts before the sample and its interfaces are approved.

OEM/ODM questions

What buyers usually ask before the first specification is written.

What is the difference between OEM and ODM here?
OEM means adapting one of our existing kiosk platforms with your branding and configuration. ODM means developing the enclosure and hardware layout to your own specification. The right route depends on size, mounting, function and volume. Before comparing factories, see how to vet a kiosk manufacturer.
What information is needed for a quotation?
Share the application, preferred reference design if any, screen size, peripherals, operating system, target country, estimated order volume and target schedule. Drawings are welcome but not required for a first discussion.
Is there a minimum order quantity?
One unit is the minimum on the standard module set. A modified cabinet starts at a small batch, and a new ODM enclosure is quoted per project because tooling and setup have to be recovered; the practical minimum for a custom run is written into the quotation.
Can a kiosk integrate my own software?
Software integration depends on the selected operating system, drivers, SDK/APIs and third-party device compatibility. Share your software stack so the requirements can be assessed.
Do all kiosk configurations support payment?
No. Payment support requires a suitable certified payment terminal or solution, payment provider arrangements and compatibility checks for the target country.
Are certifications and lead times fixed?
They depend on the actual model, components, market and scope of changes. Request documentary confirmation for certification and an agreed project schedule before ordering.
What is the difference between OEM and white label?
White label sells an existing product under your brand with no design change. OEM builds to a specification you own, which can go as far as a new cabinet, a different module set and your own software image. The practical test is who holds the drawing: in white label the factory does, in OEM you do.
How long does an ODM project take?
Production inside the standard module set runs 15 days from deposit and approved drawing. A new enclosure adds mechanical design, tooling and a prototype before that clock starts, and the total is quoted per project because it depends on how far the design moves from an existing platform.
Who owns the drawings and the CAD files?
The buyer owns the drawings and CAD files paid for in the project. The supplier keeps a licence back to build repeat orders, and the arrangement is written into the contract rather than left implicit, so a future supplier change does not mean re-drawing the product.
Can we supply our own modules?
Yes, on most projects. Send the model and the SDK, and we check platform support, the physical envelope and the supply budget before the cabinet is drawn. Customer-supplied modules keep their warranty and SDK access on your account, which is usually the point of supplying them.
Can the unit carry only our brand?
Yes. Cabinet graphics, labels, manuals and packaging can all carry your brand with no Xinyuehong marks on the product, and the shipping documents can be neutral. Agree the branding scope with the drawing, because the front graphic is applied at the same stage as the powder coat.
