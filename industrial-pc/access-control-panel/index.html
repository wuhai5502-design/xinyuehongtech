ACCESS CONTROL PANEL

Wall-Mount Access Control Intercom Panel

A silver-finished wall panel that puts a touchscreen, camera, RFID reader, fingerprint sensor and talk-to-staff buttons into one enclosure, for door entry, staff attendance and visitor calling.

The faceplate is a brushed metal sheet fixed with four corner screws over a recessed frame. The display sits on the left behind a stepped bezel; the camera is centred above it, with two slim apertures along the top edge for a card slot or a sensor. On the right are the speaker grille, the RFID reading field and a fingerprint module, with the TALK and ALARM buttons below and a microphone aperture under them. That cluster is the point of the product: a visitor can call, be seen, and be identified by card or finger at one piece of wall.

Configuration options

Points to agree before a quotation. Final availability and module compatibility depend on the access-control platform and the software that runs on it.

  • Screen size, aspect ratio and touch technology
  • Android or Windows platform image
  • Camera resolution, and infrared illumination where the entrance is dark
  • RFID protocol (13.56 MHz or IC card) or a second card slot
  • Fingerprint sensor type and the SDK supplied with it
  • Relay outputs for the door lock, door sensor and exit button
  • Alarm input, and the call route to the guard desk
  • PoE or DC power, with rear cable entry
  • Silver, black or custom finish and front branding
  • Wall bracket and cable exit position

Before you request a quotation

How is the door lock wired to the panel?
The lock hangs off a dry relay contact on the panel, normally open or normally closed depending on the lock, with a separate supply for the lock itself. A door sensor and an exit button normally join the same controller. Confirm the lock type and its current draw before the panel is specified.
Should the panel run Android or Windows?
The operating system on an access-control panel follows the access-control or intercom software you intend to run. Android suits a dedicated visitor or attendance app with a small SDK surface; Windows suits a desktop access-control client and its drivers. The platform is fixed before the panel is ordered.
Can we use our own access-control software?
Yes, if the readers are exposed through documented interfaces. Send the SDK, the API and the credential format the software expects, and we confirm which modules on the faceplate can serve it. The kiosk supplies the hardware; the access decision stays in your software.
Where are the fingerprint and card records stored?
Where fingerprint and card records are stored is a decision of the access-control system, not of the panel. Templates can sit on the panel or on a controller behind it, and the retention rule follows your local data-protection requirement. We confirm the chosen route during the specification review.
What power and network does it need?
Power over Ethernet where the chosen board supports it, otherwise a 12 or 24 V DC feed. Network is a standard RJ45, and both the power and the data cable enter from the wall behind the bracket, so the services have to be in place before the panel is hung.
