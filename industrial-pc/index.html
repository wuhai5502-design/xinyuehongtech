How to specify an industrial computer

Four questions that shape the platform, the I/O set and the mechanical design.

1. Software first

List the operating system, runtime, drivers and CPU class your application actually needs.

2. I/O and expansion

Count the serial ports, LAN ports, USB devices and expansion slots the installation requires.

3. Environment

Record ambient temperature, dust, vibration, power input range and available mounting space.

4. Lifecycle

Agree the supply period, revision control and what happens when a component reaches end of life.

What changes when the computer goes inside a kiosk

A control cabinet and a public terminal are different environments for the same hardware.

Heat, without a fan

A sealed kiosk has no airflow path, so the computer relies on conduction to the cabinet wall and on derating. Add the sun load on the front glass and the printer motor, and the internal temperature can sit 10 to 15 K above the room. A fanless platform is the right starting point; the enclosure still has to give it a path to metal.

Service access decides the layout

In a cabinet the computer sits behind a door. In a kiosk it often sits behind the display, which means the display has to come out before the SSD can be reached. Plan the service path before the brackets: what is replaced most often (paper, cards, drive) should be reachable without removing the screen.

One power supply for the whole unit

The display, the computer, the printer and the readers share a supply, and the printer motor draws more than the specification sheet suggests. Budget the peak, not the average, and prefer a platform with a wide DC input so a 24 V site does not need a second converter.

Cable routing and EMC

The printer and the card reader are the two noisy neighbours inside a kiosk. Keep the display cable away from the printer loom, ground the panel chassis to the cabinet, and use shielded cable where the run passes a motor. Most field reports of a flickering screen trace back to this run, not to the panel.

Questions buyers ask

Which platform should we start with?
Start with the software, not the hardware. List the operating system, the drivers and the SDKs the application needs; that list eliminates most platforms before price is discussed. A Windows-only payment SDK ends the Android board option immediately.
Can you supply the computer without the display?
Yes. The fanless box PC and the Android board exist for cabinets where the panel is already chosen or is supplied by another party. The display and the computer are then specified separately, with the cable run between them treated as part of the design.
What if the site runs on 24 V?
The fanless box PC accepts a 9 to 55 V DC input, which covers 12 V and 24 V sites without a second converter. The panel PC and the board are normally fed from the kiosk supply, so the voltage budget belongs in the electrical schedule rather than in the datasheet alone.
Do you load the operating system?
We load the image and the drivers for the modules in the build, and freeze the versions for repeat orders. The application itself stays with you, and the update route after handover is agreed at specification stage.
What happens when a component reaches end of life?
The supply period, revision control and last-time-buy arrangement are written into the project rather than assumed. Where a CPU or board is close to end of life, the replacement platform is qualified before the first batch ships, not after.
Is the industrial PC affected by the kiosk certification?
Yes, as part of the assembled terminal. The EMC and electrical safety of the finished unit cover the computer inside it, and the module certificates do not transfer to the kiosk by themselves. The compliance page sets out which document belongs to which party.
