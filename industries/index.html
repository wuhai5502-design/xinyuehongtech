What actually changes between industries

Four variables decide most of the bill of materials, regardless of which cabinet you start from.

01

The identity step

A bank authenticates a card and a PIN. A hospital may need an ID card, a medical insurance card and a face capture. A government hall may need a passport MRZ or a second-generation ID read. The reader set is the first thing fixed, because it changes the deck layout.

02

The payment stack

Retail and healthcare usually need a certified third-party payment terminal with its own approval. Bank branches need an encrypting PIN pad on a separate secure module. Cash handling is a cabinet-depth decision, not an option.

03

What comes out of the printer

An 80 mm thermal slip covers queue numbers, receipts and short confirmations. Medical records, certificates and application forms need an A4 module, which forces a larger cabinet and a different paper path.

04

Who is standing there

Standing adults, seated patients, wheelchair users and people in a hurry all change the screen height, the reach distance and the angle of the payment deck. That is a drawing-stage decision, not a mounting-stage one.

How to use these guides

Three steps, in this order. Doing them backwards is what turns a kiosk project into a cabinet redesign.

01

Write down the queue

Describe what the person is waiting for, not what the machine does. "Pay a consultation fee before a blood test" is a specification; "self-service payment terminal" is a shopping list.

02

Fix the identity step

Which card, document or booking reference proves who this person is? That answer selects the reader modules, and the reader modules decide the deck layout and the cabinet width.

03

Then pick the cabinet

Only now does the choice between floor-standing, wall-mounted, countertop or mobile matter. The same service can need a different cabinet in a hall than in a corridor.

If the project spans two industries — a hospital that also issues social-security certificates, a bank branch inside a government service centre — start from the union of the reader sets rather than from either guide alone. The cabinet is then drawn once, and the software decides which service appears on screen.

Questions we are asked first

One site serves two of these industries. Do we need two kiosk models?
Usually not. One cabinet can run two services if its reader bank covers both requirements, which is the normal case in a citizen hall that also takes insurance payments. The module set is specified as the union of the two, and the software decides which service is presented.
Can these terminals carry our own branding?
Yes. Cabinet colour, logo placement, front graphics and the interface are part of every OEM/ODM project; the reference photos show standard finishes only. Branding approval happens on a sample before a batch runs.
Do you supply the software that runs on the terminal?
The terminals ship as hardware plus platform — Windows, Android or Linux. The service application is yours or your integrator's, and we confirm drivers, interfaces and SDK support for the readers and printers before the cabinet is drawn.
