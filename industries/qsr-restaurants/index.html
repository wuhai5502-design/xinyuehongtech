QSR Self-Ordering Kiosks for Fast Food & Fast Casual

Lunch service is a throughput problem. At 12:30 the constraint is not the menu screen, it is how many orders a minute the counter can take. A kiosk earns its place only if it absorbs the simple orders, survives the grease and the heat, and prints a receipt without a staff member walking over.

Where a quick-service queue actually breaks

Peak concurrency is the number that matters. Between 12:00 and 13:30 a single till may take three to four orders a minute, and a queue of twelve people forms in under five. A kiosk does not need to be faster than the till; it needs to take the orders that do not need a human — the standard combo, the repeat order, the item with one modifier.

The second break is accuracy, not speed. Order errors cost more than the extra minute they save, and every modifier is a chance to mishear. A touchscreen with a clear modifier path and a printed confirmation removes that failure mode entirely.

The third is environmental, and it is the one that kills cheap units. A restaurant back-of-house runs hot, and airborne grease settles on everything. A screen that needs wiping every hour, a printer that jams because the paper path is exposed, a PC with a fan pulling that air through the cabinet — these are the reasons an installed kiosk gets switched off in month four.

What the specification has to cover

Six items that decide whether the unit is still in service next summer.

SCREEN

Size and angle

21.5 to 32 inches portrait, set at a 30-degree reading angle for a standing customer. A countertop unit suits a low counter; a floor-standing unit suits a queue that forms in front of the machine.

PAYMENT

Terminal bracket

Ingenico, Verifone and PAX devices mount on a plate cut to the specific model, with power and cable route inside the cabinet. The payment certification stays with the terminal.

PRINTING

Printer duty cycle

80 mm thermal with an auto-cutter sized for the peak hour, not the daily average. Paper changes must be a front-door operation that a shift supervisor can do in under a minute.

SCANNING

2D code reader

A deck scanner reads coupon codes and loyalty QR codes at the same station as the order screen, so the customer does not handle paper twice.

CLEANING

Grease and wipe-down

Flat glass, sealed edges and no fabric surfaces. The front face has to survive a degreaser wipe several times a day without the print wearing off.

PLATFORM

POS integration

Windows or Android, with the ordering application and its printer and payment SDKs confirmed before the cabinet is drawn. The kiosk is hardware; the POS logic stays in your stack.

Procurement checklist

  • Peak orders per hour, and how many of them are modifier-free
  • The exact payment terminal model, so the bracket is cut once
  • Receipt length and whether a second copy is printed for the kitchen
  • Counter height and available footprint — countertop or floor-standing
  • Cleaning chemicals used on the front face
  • POS or ordering platform, and who owns the integration
  • Languages on screen, and whether Arabic or another RTL language is required
  • Pilot scope: two to four units in one site before a chain roll-out

Restaurant projects move fastest when the payment terminal is already chosen. The bracket, the cable route and the printer position all depend on it, and re-cutting a fascia is the most expensive change in the project.

Questions we are asked first

Can the kiosk run our existing POS or ordering application?
Yes. The terminal ships as hardware plus platform — Windows or Android — and your ordering application runs on it. We confirm the printer, scanner and payment SDK support before the cabinet is drawn, because those decide the platform choice.
Do you support Ingenico, Verifone and PAX payment terminals?
The mounting plate and cut-out are made for the specific device model you use, with power and cable routing inside the cabinet. Send the model number and we will confirm the bracket drawing before production.
Countertop or floor-standing for a quick-service restaurant?
Countertop suits a low counter with one customer at a time and modest volume. Floor-standing suits a queue that forms in front of the unit and a peak above roughly 150 orders a day. The deciding factors are counter height and queue space, not the software.
How do you handle heat and grease in the kitchen area?
The build is fanless, so no air is pulled through the cabinet, and the front face is flat glass with sealed edges. Ambient temperature and grease exposure are confirmed at specification stage; a unit placed beside a fryer is a different case from one in the dining area.
