Retail Self-Checkout & Self-Service Kiosks

A self-checkout lane is judged on two numbers: items scanned per minute and interventions per hundred transactions. Every hardware decision — scan window, screen height, bagging space, payment position — moves one of those numbers.

Scan, pay, bag, repeat

Self-checkout fails on exceptions, not on normal baskets. A barcode that will not read, a loose item with no label, an age-restricted product — each one needs a staff member, and if the station is badly laid out the staff member has to walk around the unit to help. The cabinet layout should let one person assist two lanes without pushing past a queue.

The scan deck is the part that gets overlooked. A window that is flush with the counter, lit, and angled toward the customer cuts the number of failed scans more than a faster processor does. A 2D imager that reads phone screens matters as much as one that reads printed labels, because loyalty and payment codes now arrive on a phone.

Payment position is the other decision. Putting the terminal on the same side as the scanner keeps the customer's hands in one place; putting it on the far side forces a reach across the bagging area, which is where cards get left behind. In unattended lanes, the payment terminal is also the part most exposed to tampering, so the mounting has to be serviceable by staff but not removable by a customer.

What the specification has to settle

SCANNING

Scan window

Flush, lit and angled toward the customer. 1D and 2D reading in the same module, so printed labels and phone screens both work without a second device.

PAYMENT

Terminal position

Same side as the scanner, reachable without crossing the bagging area. The mounting plate is cut for the acquirer's device, and the cable route is inside the cabinet so it cannot be unplugged by a customer.

PRINTING

Receipt or digital

80 mm thermal with auto-cutter where a paper receipt is still expected; a QR on screen where the chain has moved to digital receipts. Both can coexist, with the printer as an option.

ERGONOMICS

Height and reach

Screen and scan deck set for a standing adult, with the payment terminal inside the same reach envelope. Trolley users and wheelchair users change the height range, and that is a drawing decision.

SECURITY

Camera and tamper

A front camera above the display supports loss prevention and age checks. In unattended lanes the payment device and the cash aperture are the parts that need the strongest mounting.

INTEGRATION

POS and loyalty

The kiosk is hardware: the POS application drives scanning, payment and printing. Loyalty lookup runs through the same application, and the scanner has to read the phone screen the loyalty code arrives on.

Procurement checklist

  • Items per basket and expected items per minute at peak
  • The exact payment device, and whether it must be customer-removable for servicing
  • Whether a paper receipt is still issued, or the chain has moved to digital
  • Bagging area dimensions and the trolley or basket flow
  • Age-restricted products, and how the station calls for authorisation
  • Loss-prevention camera requirements and where footage is stored
  • POS platform and loyalty integration owner
  • Pilot: one store, two lanes, four weeks, measured on interventions per hundred baskets

Questions we are asked first

Is this a complete self-checkout lane or only the kiosk?
The cabinet carries the screen, scanner, payment mounting, printer and camera provisions. The bagging area, the scale if the store uses one, and the POS application are specified with the store fit-out and your POS vendor rather than bundled into the kiosk.
Can the scanner read phone screens for loyalty and payment codes?
Yes, where a 2D imager is specified. Printed 1D labels and phone screens are read by the same module, which is why the 2D option matters even in stores that still print labels.
Which payment terminals can be mounted?
The plate is cut for the specific device your acquirer provides — commonly Ingenico, Verifone or PAX. Send the model and the mounting drawing and the bracket is confirmed before production.
Can the kiosk be used unattended, for example in a 24-hour store?
Yes, but the specification changes: the payment device and any cash aperture need tamper-resistant mounting, the cabinet is usually bolted down, and the camera becomes part of the design rather than an option.
