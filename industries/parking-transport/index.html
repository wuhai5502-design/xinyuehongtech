Parking Payment & Transport Kiosks

A parking payment terminal is judged on a wet Tuesday: a driver stops, pays in under a minute and drives off. Every design decision — deck angle, screen brightness, ticket slot, plinth — follows from that one moment rather than from the screen.

The payment deck is the whole design

Drivers arrive at an angle, often with the engine running. The card reader, contactless pad and ticket slot have to sit on one surface at one reach point; split them across the front face and the driver steps back, leans in, and leaves the card in the reader. That is why parking terminals are built around an angled deck rather than a vertical panel.

The screen has to survive a windscreen reflection and a low sun. Anti-glare glass and 500 to 1,000 nits are normal here, and the mounting orientation matters more than the panel specification: a screen facing east is unusable at 08:00 whatever its brightness.

The third issue is the weather. A kerbside unit sees rain at an angle, dust, salt in coastal cities and cleaning chemicals. The enclosure is sealed with a gasketed door, the cable entry is from below, and the plinth is bolted so the unit cannot be pushed out of alignment with the lane.

What a parking specification has to settle

PAYMENT

Contactless and QR

Card and phone payment through a certified terminal, plus a QR scanner for app-based parking. Both sit on the deck, inside one reach envelope.

TICKETS

Issue and collect

Some sites issue a ticket at entry and collect at exit; others are ticketless. The printer outlet and the scanner position follow from which model the operator runs.

ENCLOSURE

Rain, dust, salt

A sealed front, gasketed door and bottom cable entry. Coastal sites need stainless fixings; inland sites can use coated steel with a plinth.

READABILITY

Sun and glare

Anti-glare glass, 500 to 1,000 nits, and an orientation chosen so the screen is not facing the rising or setting sun.

FIXING

Bolt-down plinth

A payment terminal is an obvious target for being struck or pushed. The plinth is bolted, and the service door is on the side away from the traffic lane.

INTEGRATION

Parking system

The kiosk talks to the parking management system for tariffs and validation. Offline behaviour — what happens when the link drops — is a software decision that has to be agreed.

Procurement checklist

  • Kerbside, lane-side or in a station hall, and whether the position is covered
  • Ticketless operation or ticket issue and collection
  • Payment methods: contactless, chip, QR, cash
  • Tariff and validation logic in the parking system, and offline behaviour
  • Power and network at the plinth, and who provides the civil works
  • Sun exposure and the direction the screen faces
  • Cleaning regime and the chemicals used on the front face
  • Service model: who clears a jam, who restocks paper, and the response time

Questions we are asked first

Is the terminal suitable for an uncovered car park?
The enclosure is specified for covered kerbside and lane-side positions as standard, with a weather-resistant front option. Fully exposed sites need a different sealing and drainage arrangement, which is confirmed against the installation drawing rather than assumed.
Can it work without tickets?
Yes. Ticketless operations use the QR scanner or the payment card as the identifier, and the printer becomes optional. The choice changes the deck layout, so it is fixed before the drawing.
How is the unit fixed to the ground?
A floor plinth with a bolt-down option for permanent positions. A free-standing base is possible for temporary sites, but it is not the default because a payment terminal is an obvious target for being struck.
What happens when the network drops?
That is a software decision: the terminal can hold a service message and direct drivers to another exit, or the parking system can support an offline mode with a stored tariff. The hardware exposes the state; the policy is agreed with the operator.
Can it take cash as well as cards?
A cash acceptor can be fitted, but it changes the cabinet depth, the service access and the certification scope. Many urban sites run card and QR only, which keeps the unit smaller and cheaper to maintain.
