Holographic display / TRANSPARENT 3D

3D Naked-Eye Holographic Display Cabinets

Transparent display cabinets with a naked-eye 3D layer, from a 10.1-inch countertop pod to a 100-inch atrium installation. Cylindrical or rectangular, with the player, the lighting and the content pipeline specified as one unit rather than a panel dropped into a box.

The cabinet is the part that decides whether the effect works. A transparent panel in a bright lobby loses contrast to the wall behind it, so the enclosure, the backing, the lighting and the standing position all have to be designed together. That is the part we build.

Three build types

Cylindrical pod. A 360-degree glass cylinder on a lit base, with the image floating in the centre. This is the format in the photo above: LED ring at the top and bottom, a character or product rendered inside, and an optional voice or gesture layer for reception and exhibition use. Available from 21.5 inches upward.

Rectangular cabinet. The retail workhorse. A transparent panel in the door, the physical product on a lit shelf behind it, graphics over the glass. Sizes from 10.1 to 86 inches, wall or plinth mounted.

Large-format wall. Above 86 inches the transparent panel gives way to LED film or projection, so the build becomes a structure: sections, freight, rigging and a service plan. We quote these as installation projects rather than cabinets.

Where it is used

  • Retail counters and window displays: jewellery, watches, cosmetics, limited editions
  • Museums and visitor centres: artefacts behind glass with context layered over them
  • Hotel and office lobbies: a virtual concierge or brand character in a pod
  • Exhibition stands: the 65 to 100-inch formats draw a crowd for the length of a show
  • Automotive and industrial showrooms: cutaway components rotating in place
  • Banks and premium retail: lift-and-learn interaction on high-value items

What to send us

Four things shorten the quotation: the object or character to be shown, the size you want, the light level where it will stand, and who owns the content. With those we can name the panel route, the cabinet depth and the realistic lead time. Without them any supplier can only quote a range.

Common questions

Is the display actually transparent, or is it a projection?
Whether an apparent-hologram display is transparent or projected depends on the size of the build. Below 32 inches most units use a transparent LCD, which lets roughly a tenth of the background light through. From 43 inches upward a transparent OLED is the better route at around 40 percent. At 100 inches the practical answer is transparent LED film or projection onto a coated film, because a transparent panel at that size does not exist commercially.
Why does the image look weaker in a bright room?
Because a transparent display has no black level of its own. Every dark pixel is whatever sits behind the unit, so ambient light lifts the blacks and the contrast falls. Under 300 lux the effect is convincing. In a 750-lux retail aisle the cabinet needs a shroud or a darker backing panel, and the transparency drops as you compensate.
Can we supply our own 3D content?
Yes, provided the asset has depth rather than being a flat render. A CAD model with materials, a photogrammetry scan or a purpose-built 3D scene all work. If you only have product photography, a modeller has to rebuild the object, and that is usually the longest lead time in the project rather than the cabinet itself.
What sizes are available?
Standard builds from 10.1 to 100 inches diagonal. The form factor changes with size: countertop pods and slab cabinets at the small end, cylindrical 360-degree showcases in the middle, and floor-standing or suspended structures from 65 inches up.
